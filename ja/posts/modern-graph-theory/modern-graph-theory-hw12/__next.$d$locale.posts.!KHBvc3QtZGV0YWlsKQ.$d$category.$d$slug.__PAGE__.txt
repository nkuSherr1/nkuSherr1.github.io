**Problem 1.**

Prove: Every Sperner-colored simplicial subdivision contains an odd number of
rainbow cells.

**Proof.**

Let

$$
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_d),
$$

where

$$
v_i has color i, and let \mathcal{T} be a Sperner-colored simplicial
subdivision of \Delta. We use induction on d.

The case d=0 is immediate. For d=1, the subdivision is an interval whose
endpoints have colors 0 and 1. Each rainbow edge is a place where the color
changes. Since the colors at the two ends are different, the number of such
changes is odd.

Assume now that d\geq 2 and that the result holds in dimension d-1. Let F
be the facet of \Delta opposite v_d. The restriction of \mathcal{T} to F
is a Sperner-colored subdivision of a (d-1)-simplex with colors
0,1,\ldots,d-1. By induction, it contains an odd number of rainbow
(d-1)-cells.

Define a graph \Gamma whose vertices are the d-cells of \mathcal{T},
together with one extra vertex *. For every (d-1)-cell with color set
$$

\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\},

$$
add an edge to \Gamma. If the cell is shared by two d-cells, join the
corresponding vertices. If it is a boundary cell, join its unique containing
d-cell to *.

Every boundary cell counted above lies in F. Indeed, if it lies in the facet
of \Delta opposite v_j, then the Sperner condition allows only colors from
\{0,1,\ldots,d\}\setminus\{j\}. Since the cell uses all the colors
0,1,\ldots,d-1, we must have j=d. Therefore
$$

\deg_{\Gamma}(*) \equiv 1 \pmod 2

$$
by the induction hypothesis.

Let T be a d-cell of \mathcal{T}. Its degree in \Gamma is the number of
its facets with color set \{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}.

If T is rainbow, exactly one facet is counted, namely the one obtained by
deleting the vertex of color d. Thus \deg_{\Gamma}(T)=1.

If T uses every color 0,1,\ldots,d-1 but does not use color d, then exactly
one of these colors is repeated among the d+1 vertices of T. Deleting either
copy of the repeated color gives a counted facet, so \deg_{\Gamma}(T)=2.

In all remaining cases, T misses at least one color from 0,1,\ldots,d-1, so
no facet is counted and \deg_{\Gamma}(T)=0.

Thus the odd-degree vertices of \Gamma, other than *, are exactly the rainbow
d-cells. By the handshaking lemma, \Gamma has an even number of odd-degree
vertices. Since * has odd degree, the number of rainbow d-cells is odd.

∎
$$
